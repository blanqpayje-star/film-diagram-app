# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Filmmakers at both student and professional levels. They use Film Diagram Studio during pre-production and post-production to build shot, lighting, and camera breakdowns; preserve reference guides; and support teaching.

## Product Purpose

Film Diagram Studio is a free, browser-only tool for creating production diagrams that help users plan, communicate, document, and teach camera and lighting decisions. Success means professional-quality pre-visualization is accessible without requiring paid specialist software.

## Positioning

An open-source, openly accessible freemium alternative to Shot Designer and Cadrage: a personal project that makes professional-grade diagramming available to filmmakers without paywall-driven feature access.

## Operating Context

Users create and revisit diagrams as part of production planning, shot breakdowns, post-production reference, collaboration guides, and teaching materials. The tool runs in a web browser and saves work locally.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Browser-only application; no native application is in scope.
- Supports scene-based camera, light, actor, prop, architectural, and furniture diagrams.
- Provides configurable camera FOV and lighting-spread cones, transforms, grid/snapping, export/import, and local persistence.
- Must remain performant and easy to use while pursuing a professional standard of interface and assets.

## Brand Commitments

The interface should speak first to filmmakers while remaining approachable to a general audience. Professional-quality assets and design are required, but not at the expense of performance or clarity.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository includes the working React application and its built-in SVG equipment assets in `src/components/ElementIcon.tsx`. No external brand kit, customer testimonials, or production imagery has been confirmed; future work must not fabricate them.

## Product Principles

1. Put capable pre-visualization tools within reach of every filmmaker.
2. Treat diagrams as working production documents, not decorative canvases.
3. Keep powerful controls direct, legible, and fast under real production pressure.
4. Make professional craft accessible without making the interface exclusive or opaque.
5. Preserve browser-first performance and independence from paid software ecosystems.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The product must remain approachable to general audiences as well as filmmaking specialists. Specific accessibility compliance targets remain open.
